# Tiny King: phase two, a claim to the crown

Status: separate design proposal, 30 October 2026. No implementation is authorised by this document. Chris authorised phase-one work and feedback changes. Phase two remains deferred.

## The proposed aim

Work your way up until you take your rightful place as king. Bring the whole land under your rule through the people who live there. A castle and an established household help other nobles take you seriously.

Chris described this direction as similar to Elderlight, an idea he discussed in an older blog post. This draft records his description. It does not claim to reproduce that post.

The current design centres on an open-ended life as a stranger, neighbour and member of a family. Phase two adds a possible long-term political aim. Coronation can mark an achievement while the world and family continue.

## Progress through people and places

The proposed progression starts with earning a place in a village. You gain trust, join the community and accept duties in exchange for shared resource rights. These duties and rights give village membership a practical meaning.

You then establish or inherit a household with enough resources and standing to support a larger estate. Marriage, inheritance and service can connect your family to established landholders. These remain relationships with named people who have their own interests.

A substantial home can develop into a hall and later a castle. This is a candidate progression, not an agreed building list. The place demonstrates your resources and ability to maintain a household.

Other landholders can then accept your authority. An oath of allegiance means a person agrees to serve a ruler under stated obligations. Further allegiances extend your rule until the recognised landholders across the realm acknowledge your crown.

This progression describes a route to discuss. It does not require every character to follow an identical sequence of titles.

## Castles and recognition

Chris wants a castle or similar seat of power to help impress other nobles. A castle gives political ambition a visible place on the map. Its construction and upkeep need a credible source of labour and resources.

The design proposal is that nobles judge both your standing and their relationship with you. A castle demonstrates standing, but it does not force another person to trust you. Their family history, interests and existing allegiances still matter.

A functioning seat can host meetings, guests and celebrations. Those uses connect the building to the social game. Exact facilities, costs and noble requirements remain undecided.

## What ruling the land means

Control means recognised authority over land through its households and rulers. Their people keep their identities, possessions and daily lives. A ruler gains defined rights and owes defined duties.

A village joining your rule does not automatically make every private store yours. Shared resources, household property and political authority need distinct rules. Earlier promises and existing family claims remain part of the world.

Allegiances can create obligations between rulers and their followers. Possible obligations include assistance, protection and contributions to a household. Their exact form remains open, along with how people respond to broken promises.

## History supports the ambition

The settlement feedback provides a foundation for this phase. Routes cross mountain passes and connect regions. Households settle near useful routes and resources, then villages develop meeting places, pubs and trades.

Successful households expand over generations. Political relationships can develop alongside that growth. On arrival, the player encounters existing estates, family claims and allegiances with a recorded past.

The same history can supply reasons for support or opposition. A relative once helped your family, a predecessor broke an oath, or two houses dispute an inheritance. Such claims need actual events, not invented biography text.

## Decisions still open

The meaning of a rightful claim needs a decision. It can come from ancestry, recognition, marriage, election or achievements during play. No secret royal ancestry is assumed by this draft.

The realm needs a clear extent before controlling the whole land becomes a completion condition. This includes the role of islands and distant regions. No new map limits are specified here.

The role of force also remains open. This proposal does not approve combat, armies or sieges. We need to decide whether political rule can pass entirely through consent, marriage and inheritance.

Other open questions include the title hierarchy, the cost of a castle and the obligations attached to allegiance. We also need to decide how succession affects recognised authority. Coronation can conclude the main ambition or begin a continuing reign.

## Boundary with the current work

This document preserves a possible second phase. It adds no tasks to the current build order and changes no existing mechanics. Phase one remains focused on people, settlements, village belonging and family succession.

Before implementation, agree the route to the crown and the smallest complete political interaction. Then write separate worker briefs and acceptance criteria. The existing review, testing and deployment rules still apply.
